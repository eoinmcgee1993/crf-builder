Modifications, and what each one does to the fuel map

Written because our own ECU builder offered a "PCX150 injector upgrade" for a CRF250L, and it was wrong in a way that could have cost somebody an engine. This page shows the working.

1. The injector mistake, in full

A fuel injector is rated by flow — how much fuel it passes per minute at a given pressure. Bigger number, more fuel per millisecond of injector opening.

InjectorFlowHolesSold as an upgrade for
PCX150 (16450-B06-00H)140 cc/min6Small-displacement Hondas — Grom, Wave, Click
Yuminashi C-type235 cc/min12CRF250L, CBR250R, CBR300R

Yuminashi D-type270 cc/min12CRF250L, CBR250R, CBR300R

MeasuredFlow figures are the manufacturers' bench ratings, listed on the parts themselves.

The upgrade path for a CRF250L starts at 235 cc/min. The PCX150 injector is 140 cc/min — around 40% less. It is not an upgrade for this engine in any sense. It is a substantial downgrade in flow.

Why the error is easy to make

The PCX150 injector is a real upgrade — for a Grom, a Wave or a Click. Those are 110–125cc engines, and 140 cc/min is a meaningful step up for them. The swap is popular enough that it is discussed constantly on Grom forums. Somebody took a well-known upgrade path for a 125 and applied it to a 250.

PrincipleFuel demand scales with power, not with what fits the fuel rail. An engine making roughly 18 hp needs materially more fuel per minute than one making roughly 13, at the same air/fuel ratio. Parts sharing a connector does not make them interchangeable.

Why it is dangerous rather than merely useless

Injector pulse width and injector flow multiply together to give the fuel delivered. A map calculated for one flow rate, run on an injector with a different one, delivers the wrong fuelling by exactly that ratio.

Fit a smaller injector and keep the map: the engine runs lean. Lean mixture burns hotter and is far more prone to detonation, and detonation is what melts piston crowns and burns exhaust valves. Add the ignition advance in a performance map and a free-flowing exhaust — both of which already push in that direction — and you have assembled the exact conditions for engine damage.

This is why our builder now offers the stock injector only, and says plainly that any other injector makes every fuel number in the file wrong.

If you do fit a bigger injector

PrincipleThe base fuel map must be scaled by the inverse of the flow ratio. Go from a 185 cc/min injector to a 260, and every pulse width needs multiplying by roughly 185/260, about 0.71, before you flash it — then verified on a wideband, because injectors are not perfectly linear at short pulse widths and the correction is only a starting point.

MeasuredThe stock injector on the CRF250L (2013–2018) is Honda part 16450-MFE-641, shared with the CBR250R, CBR300R, CB300F and Rebel 300.

UnverifiedIts rated flow we could not confirm. A figure of 240 cc/min circulates on forums, but the poster who gave it was himself questioning it, the thread it comes from is now paywalled, and it sits badly against the fact that the smallest injector sold as an upgrade for this engine is 235 cc/min — which would make the upgrade a downgrade. One of those two numbers is wrong and we cannot tell you which. So we publish no scaling factor: the ratio above is an illustration of the method, not a number to flash. If you have the figure from a Honda service manual or a bench test, send it and we will finish this section properly.

Bigger is not automatically better either. Oversize the injector and the pulse widths at idle and small throttle openings get so short that metering accuracy falls apart, which is why the sensible ceiling is the smallest injector that still keeps duty cycle within roughly 80–85% at peak demand.

2. What actually needs the map changed

The useful division is not "performance parts" versus "cosmetic". It is whether the modification changes how much air the engine swallows, or how much fuel a given command delivers. Those need a remap. Everything else does not.

ModificationRemap needed?Why
Slip-on silencerWorth it, not criticalModest change in flow. The closed-loop system trims some of it at cruise; the open-loop areas at wide throttle are where it shows.
Full exhaust systemYesHeader and silencer together change flow enough to lean out wide-open throttle noticeably.
Open airbox / high-flow filterYesMore air for the same fuel command is a lean shift, in the same direction as the exhaust.
Different injectorMandatoryChanges the fuel delivered per millisecond. Nothing compensates for this automatically.
Big-bore kitMandatoryMore displacement, more air, more fuel needed everywhere. This is a full retune, not a correction.
CamYesChanges where and how the engine breathes; the shape of the map changes, not just its scale.
Gearing, suspension, wheels, barsNoNothing to do with fuelling.
Graphics kitNoIncluded because someone always asks.

PrincipleEvery row above follows from whether the change moves air or moves fuel. None of it is brand-specific.

3. Baseline, and what to expect

MeasuredIndependent dyno testing puts a stock CRF250L in the region of 18 hp at the wheel, with the 2017 revision picking up roughly 2 hp over the earlier engine.

VendorSellers of exhaust, intake and ECU packages commonly claim around a 15% gain when the three are combined. Treat that as marketing until you have your own dyno sheet: it is a seller's figure for a best-case combination, not an independent measurement of any one part.

The honest summary of a 250 dual-sport: exhaust and intake alone move very little without fuelling to match, the fuelling is where the driveability gain actually lives, and no bolt-on turns this engine into a different class of motorcycle. What it does do is clean up the flat spots the factory left in to pass emissions.
